What watermarking is

Watermarking is a family of techniques intended to add or preserve signals about content origin, generation, editing, or provenance. Signals may be visible, metadata-based, cryptographic, platform-specific, or absent from plain pasted text.

What this Lite tool can check

It can highlight visible disclosures, provenance-style notes users paste, metadata hints users report, repeated phrasing patterns, and review questions for a human reviewer.

What this Lite tool cannot prove

It cannot verify hidden markers, inspect original files, validate C2PA manifests, identify authorship, or replace a trusted provenance system.

Why missing signals are limited

Absence of a visible signal is not proof of human authorship. A signal may have been removed, never added, or lost during copy/paste, export, or platform processing.

Why suspicious patterns are limited

Formulaic language or repeated phrasing can appear in human writing, templates, edited drafts, and generated text. Treat patterns as review notes, not authorship decisions.
